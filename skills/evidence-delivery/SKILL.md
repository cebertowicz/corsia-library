# Evidence delivery

No test results yet: this has one real run behind it, and nobody has measured whether it beats doing without. Use it when a recurring report or source-based document needs more than two substantive steps. If current sources already answer the question, skip the optional research and say why.

The accountable owner keeps interpretation, scope, recommendation and acceptance. Workers may gather bounded evidence or assemble a specified document. A review request grants no authority to act on its findings.

## Applying these steps

1. **Source contract — accountable owner.** Record the audience, the question, the intended use, the requested scope, primary sources and their dates, missing evidence, how you'll know it's complete, and any limits on your authority. Name the strongest plausible counterexample. Pick the one output document and reuse what already exists. Don't let an example narrow the whole request.
2. **Research — owner or a bounded worker, when needed.** Search the question and the concept beneath it. Seek primary sources and counterevidence, and record selection limits and contradictions. Respect access limits, rate limits and approved spending. An empty search doesn't prove absence, and a source's recommendation doesn't prove a benefit in your case.
3. **Assemble — owner or a bounded worker.** Separate observations, judgments and unknowns. Keep the owner's reasoning. Tie material claims to sources, calculations to inputs, and approvals to the words that gave them. Work in the one output document and keep unrelated edits.
4. **Verify — accountable owner, plus an independent checker when warranted.** Check every requested part against its sources and the strongest counterexample. Check calculations, links and the actual format, and open or render it when appearance matters. Remove or qualify unsupported claims, and send defects back to the step that caused them. Ask what a skilled practitioner would notice that these checks miss, and turn any material answer into a check for this run.
5. **Deliver — accountable owner.** Put the verified document where its audience can reach it, update any index it belongs to, and hand it over through the usual channel. Keep draft, send and publish distinct. Record remaining gaps, returned work, corrections and known total cost; unknown cost stays unknown. Delivering it doesn't mean the recipient accepted it.

If a source, tool or permission is missing, report it and hold the affected step; never substitute silently. Ask only for facts or choices your sources can't settle.

## Prove the claim as stated

Pick evidence that shows the outcome the claim names, never a convenient proxy. "It did useful work without follow-up prompts" needs evidence of that work, not elapsed time or a screenshot of a clock. Keep the proof to what was asked; it isn't a new campaign.

## Judging a run

Look at recipient acceptance and corrections, claim or format failures that got through, attention spent, and total cost including rework. Keep the person's questions and limits when you revise this. Merge or retire it if it adds overhead without improving results.
